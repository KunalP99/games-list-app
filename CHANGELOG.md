## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **A profile you make your own (public edition).** Your profile now opens with a full-width card in your colour: your picture, name, @username, a short bio, your level and XP bar, and your total hours played and games. It never moves. Under it are cards you choose: Currently playing, Recently completed, Up next, Your year (hours per month), Finish rate, Achievements, Favourite genres, Platforms, Play streak and Completed this year. Cards come in three sizes, on a four-column grid: Full (a whole row), Half and Square (a quarter, so the number cards sit four to a row; two to a row on phones); some come in more than one. **Edit profile** lets you drag cards into any order (mouse, touch, or the keyboard: Space, arrows, Space), click an empty space to add a card, switch a card between its sizes, remove one, and write your bio; Done saves and Cancel puts everything back. A Shop button in the edit bar is there for later and doesn't do anything yet. Visitors see your cards apart from the ones your privacy settings hide. Your games list has moved to the profile's **Games** tab, and **Profile** is now first in the nav. The friends edition's profile is unchanged. (SQL: `db/profile-cards.sql`, both projects. Code: `lib/profile-cards.ts`, `components/profile/v2/`.)
- **Groundwork for tracking games played in PlayStation emulators.** The site can now answer the desktop app when it sees a game running in DuckStation, PCSX2, RPCS3, PPSSPP, Vita3K or shadPS4. The app sends the console, the disc's serial code and the name the emulator shows, and the site:
  - **you own it:** tracks it and marks it Emulated;
  - **you own it but it's Completed, Backlog or DNF:** offers to replay or resume it, as usual;
  - **an exact match you don't own:** asks "Track it?", which adds it as Playing, marked Emulated, with its console as the platform;
  - **anything else:** shows the best three matches for that console, plus "None of these".

  Your answer is remembered per console and disc, so the same game is never asked about twice. "None of these" stops the questions for that game. Nothing changes until a desktop app release uses it. (SQL: `db/emulator-tracking.sql`, both projects. Code: `lib/emulators.ts`, `lib/agent-emulator.ts`, `/api/agent/emulator-choice`.)
- **Search every game, and a page for each (public edition).** A search bar under the dashboard greeting searches all of IGDB as you type (↑ ↓ Enter Esc; games you have are marked "In your library"). Each result opens a store-style page: the trailer and screenshots, IGDB's critic and player scores, the Steam review summary, release date, developer, age rating (PEGI, else ESRB), who can play (single player, co-op, split screen), platforms and tags, which friends have it (click for their status and hours; their privacy settings apply), where to buy it, Add to Backlog or I'm playing it, DLC and editions (a text list with DLC/Edition tags, small extras behind "N more DLC"), and more from the developer. A game that isn't out yet gets a countdown (or "Coming 2027" when only the year is known), per-platform release dates and pre-order links. IGDB data is cached for an hour per game. (`/discover/<igdbId>`, `lib/store-page.ts`, `getStoreGame` in `lib/igdb.ts`.)
- **Friends, in the public edition.** Tap **+** on the dashboard's Friends card to search for people by username or name and send a request; requests to you appear at the top of the card with Accept and Decline, and once accepted you're friends both ways. Friends see each other's playing now, play sessions, head-to-head stats, activity and tickets (each person's privacy settings still apply). A profile has a friend button and a ⋯ menu to remove a friend or block someone; blocking ends any friendship, stops requests both ways and hides you from their search. With no friends yet, the card says so. The friends edition is unchanged: everyone there is still a friend. (SQL: `db/friends.sql`. The public project needs it; on the friends project it's housekeeping, any time before the next `db:dump`.)
- **Friends card shows each friend's level (public edition).** Under their name, instead of how many games they've completed and are playing. The friends edition keeps the game counts.
- **Right-click a friend to remove them (public edition).** On the dashboard's Friends card, right-click a friend (or use the ⋯ that appears on hover, and always on touch screens) for View profile and Remove friend, the same menu style as the game cards.
- **Settings → Privacy (public edition).** Choose Everyone, Friends or Only me for your profile, games, achievements, play activity and tickets (defaults: the first three Everyone, the last two Friends), and manage the people you've blocked. The database enforces the choices, not just the pages; a private profile shows its banner in the person's colour (no picture, no figures: just their picture, name, @username and level) and a "This profile is private" card with a lock and an Add friend button.
- **Deleting a game shows what it costs in tickets.** The confirmation (on a game's page and in a card's ⋯ / right-click menu) shows a small chip with the tickets you'll lose and your balance before and after, e.g. "−3 · Balance 1 → −2". A balance can go below zero, as before. Deleting a DNF game shows the 5 tickets coming back in green (friends edition); a game that earned nothing shows no chip. The game page's Delete button now uses the same dialog instead of an inline "Are you sure?". Both editions. (`deleteImpact` in `app/(app)/games/actions.ts`; `deleteWarning` / `deleteTicketChange` in `lib/game-quick-actions.ts`.)
- **Groundwork for a public edition.** The same code can now run as a second, separate site for the public, with its own database. Nothing changes on this site: it stays the friends edition by default. The public edition has no wheel (no Wheel page, history, settings, achievements or dashboard card), and its name is a placeholder until it has a real one. It also has a single Playing status instead of Playing + Ambient: games the tracker finds are added as Playing, there's no Now Playing section, and Recently played shows every Playing game the tracker has seen (hidden for anyone not using the tracker), with a Playing tab in the games list for the rest. Marking a game as DNF never costs tickets in the public edition. The public edition's nav has no History menu; the ticket history page is still there by its address, and shows only your own tickets. In place of the wheel card, its dashboard has a bold **Your level** card: your level and XP bar, the achievement you're closest to unlocking (with what's left, e.g. "9h to go"), and a link to all achievements. Sign-up is built for strangers: you **choose your username** (checked live as you type, with suggestions when it's taken; changeable once every 30 days in Settings, and old profile links keep working), passwords need 8+ characters with a letter and a number, a Cloudflare Turnstile check stops bots, new accounts confirm their email, there's **Forgot password?**, and you can sign in with **Google** or **Discord** (picking a username the first time). Two people whose emails start the same way can no longer block each other from signing up. Profile banners show a single ticket figure, the tickets you have now, instead of "total (balance)". Completing a game earns tickets and XP only from what the site can check: games picked from search, completed since you joined, with longer games earning more when the desktop app tracked the hours (a game without tracked time earns 1 ticket, up to 5 times a day), and no 100% or sequel bonus for now; the game's page says what it earned and why. Your ticket history, play sessions and wheel history are visible only to you.
- **Quick actions on the games list.** Every card (grid and cover views, and the Recently Played strip) shows a **⋯** button in its top-left corner when you hover over it, and every table row has one at the end. On phones and tablets the button is always visible, since they have no hover. **Right-clicking** a card or row opens the same menu. Shift + right-click still gives the browser's own menu, for "Open in new tab". The menu offers:
  - **Edit**;
  - **Add to / Remove from favourites**;
  - a move to every other status: Mark as Completed…, Move to Playing / Backlog / Ambient, Mark as DNF…;
  - **Delete…**.

  Only your own games get the menu; a friend's profile has none. Tickets work exactly as they do from the edit page:
  - **Completed** opens the usual Mark as Completed form.
  - Anything that costs tickets asks first: leaving Completed takes back the completion tickets, and DNF applies the penalty.
  - Delete confirms and keeps you on the list.

  The rules live in `lib/game-quick-actions.ts` (tested); the menu is `components/games/GameActionsMenu.tsx`; there are new `setGameStatus` and `setFavourite` server actions. The Mark as Completed form is now one component (`CompleteGameModal`), shared by the card button, the detail page and the menu.

- **A new dashboard for everyone.** It replaces the old one outright; there's no switch back. Gone with the old dashboard: the games-per-year and genre/platform charts, the anticipated-games and recommendations carousels, and the separate events and "Playing now" sections (events now sit in "Coming up", and live friends in the Friends card). The new layout:
  - The main column opens on a large artwork card for the game you played most recently, whether it's on Playing or Ambient, with a live timer while the tracker sees it. A Playing game gets "Mark as completed" and "Open"; an Ambient one gets Open only, since Ambient games never complete.
  - Below that are four stats (Games, Hours played, Finish rate, and Tickets with how many you earned this week, all in the same Anton display font as the Stats page's big numbers) and one Activity feed for you and your friends, with Everyone / You / Friends tabs (10 items per tab).
  - The right column has your wheel turn, with where you are in the Spin → Spin + Reroll → Choose cycle.
  - It also has your friends, where anyone the tracker sees in a game right now is pinned to the top with the game and a ticking timer, refreshed every minute.
  - "Your week" shows your tracked play over the last 7 days: a bar per day (today in your accent), the total, your most played game, and how many of the days you played. The arrow next to the title switches it to "Your month" (the last 30 days, a wide bar per day with its hours on top) or "Your year" (the last 12 calendar months, this one so far, a bar per month with its hours on top). Both scroll sideways (drag, swipe or mouse wheel) and opens on today. Hover or tap a bar to see that day or month. The bars grow up one by one, left to right, each time you open the dashboard or switch period (skipped if your device asks for reduced motion). The card is hidden if nothing was tracked all year.
  - Last in the right column is "Coming up": backlog games releasing soon, mixed with gaming events.
  - A small 🔥 streak pill beside the date counts the days in a row you've played. It's orange once you've played today, and grey with a "play today to keep it" nudge if your last session was yesterday.

### Fixed
- **Accented game names match their plain spelling.** "Okami" now matches IGDB's "Ōkami", and "Pokemon" matches "Pokémon", when the desktop app identifies a game by its name (Epic, EA, emulators). The accent used to make the letter disappear from the comparison, so no exact match was ever found. (`normaliseTitle` in `lib/search-ranking.ts`.)
- **The tracker no longer adds the base game when you own an edition of it.** Running "Grand Theft Auto IV" on Steam while owning "GTA IV: Complete Edition" filed a second game, because Steam's app maps to the base game on IGDB. It now counts as the edition you own, whichever way round and however many steps apart (The Witcher 3's 10th Anniversary Edition is an edition of the Complete Edition, itself an edition of the game) (IGDB's version links, `getEditionFamily`; DLC and expansions are never treated as the same game), and the edition gets the Steam app id so later launches match straight away. That includes when the base game is already in your library as Completed (or dropped, or in the backlog): an edition you're playing now takes the hours, instead of a replay of the completed game being offered. A duplicate already made can be merged with `db/merge-tracker-duplicate.sql`.
- **Clicking a tab straight after searching (or as the page opens) now sticks.** On your games list and the Videos page, a tab or sort clicked within a moment of typing in the search box, or of the page appearing, was quietly undone and you were put back where you were. The tab now sticks and keeps your search.
- **Every backlog game moves to Playing when it wins the wheel.** A backlog game that wasn't linked to a game from search (added by hand or imported without a match) could win and quietly stay in your backlog. It now moves to Playing like any other win.
- **Changing your password asks for your current one**, so a session left open on someone else's screen can't be used to take over your account. Accounts made with Google or Discord, which have no password yet, can still set one without it.
- **Safer by default:** the site can no longer be shown inside other websites (a trick used to make people click things without knowing), and browsers are told to use only secure connections and not to share more than needed about where you came from.
- **Limits on game searches and lookups:** each account can make up to 60 game searches and 120 game lookups a minute (and the desktop app 30 identifications), so no one account can use up the game database's quota for everyone.
- **A play session the desktop app never closed no longer shows as "– now" forever.** If the app was restarted, crashed or the PC slept mid-game, the activity feed (dashboard, Activity page and profiles) kept that session running indefinitely and added "playing now" to the day's row. It now ends at the last time the app checked in, matching the game's own Play Sessions list, and a new session of the same game on the same PC closes the old one properly.
- **Searching for a title with a comma or brackets works.** Searching your games (or a friend's) for "Warhammer 40,000" or "Ratchet (2016)" used to fail and show nothing. Search now always looks for exactly what you typed, including `%` and `_`.
- **Tickets can only be earned from your own games.** Saving an edit with a game that wasn't yours could still hand out completion tickets and XP. It now just says the game wasn't found.
- **Clear messages for impossible hours and achievement figures.** Negative hours, or achievement progress outside 0–100%, are refused with a message instead of being saved (and counted towards tickets). There's no practical cap on hours: anything up to 99,999.9 (the most the database can store) is fine, so a game with 10,000+ hours saves as normal.
- **"Same game" matching is exact.** Finding your replays of a game, friends who've played it, and a wheel win already in your library no longer treats a `_` or `%` in a title as a wildcard.
- **Safer handling of a game's DLC and video lists.** A damaged list no longer stops a game saving, and only real YouTube videos with secure thumbnails are kept.
- **The desktop app's Download button is less likely to fail.** The latest release is looked up at most every five minutes instead of on every click, staying well inside GitHub's limits.
- **Sign-up and password-reset emails always link to this site.** On the live site, the link address comes from the site's own settings rather than from the request.
- **Double-clicks and retries can't count twice.** Completing, dropping or moving a game, saving an edit, and every way of finishing a wheel turn now happen once even if the request arrives twice (a double-click, a slow connection retrying, two tabs). Before, completing twice could award the tickets twice, and accepting a spin twice could skip a whole turn. The second request now changes nothing and says the game or turn was already changed.
- **Paid wheel actions can't overspend.** The balance is checked and the tickets taken in one step, so two paid spins at once can't both go through on tickets you only have once.
- **Tracked hours can't be counted twice.** If the desktop app sends the same update twice at once, its hours are only added once, and two PCs tracking at the same time can't overwrite each other's hours.
- **Achievements and their XP are awarded once.** Achievements can no longer be removed or added by hand from the browser, which allowed the same achievement's XP to be earned over and over.
- **Taking back a completion only takes back completion tickets**, and only once even if it happens twice at the same moment.
- **Reserved usernames are refused however you sign up**, not just through the sign-up form.
- **Private notes are truly private.** Notes marked private were hidden on the page but could still be read by other signed-in users behind the scenes. They're now stored separately, where only you can read them.
- **Completion XP is taken back when a game stops being completed**, like its tickets already were. Moving a game from Completed to Playing and back used to add its XP again every time.
- **Wheel history can't be edited.** It's recorded by the site itself, so a turn can't be quietly removed from your history.
- **The desktop app's "Add device" no longer creates a sign-in key.** It was a way for a stolen key to make more of itself. To add a PC, open the app on it and click Connect, as before.
- **No more "Mark Complete" button on game cards.** It crowded out each card's stats (hours, rating, achievement %), which now always show in full. Completing a game is still in every card's ⋯ menu and right-click menu, on the game's own page, and on the dashboard banner.
- **Game cards are all the same height again.** In the cover view, a card with more stats (hours, rating, achievement %) let them wrap onto a second line and squeezed "Mark Complete" onto two lines, making that card taller than its neighbours. The stats and button now stay on one line, and cards in a row share one height with their stats lined up along the bottom.
- **Game search no longer needs the punctuation.** Half-typed titles without their colon or apostrophe used to find nothing: "hunt sh" missed Hunt: Showdown 1896, "baldurs g" missed Baldur's Gate III, "marvels spi" missed Marvel's Spider-Man. The search now also matches on IGDB's punctuation-free slug, and the ranking treats "baldurs" as "Baldur's" and "spiderman" as "Spider-Man". Applies everywhere a game is searched (add/edit form, wheel, admin).
- **Picking a game from a search dropdown no longer reopens it.** Choosing a suggestion copies its name into the box, which ran the search again and popped the list straight back open; a search still in flight when you clicked could do the same. Fixed in the game form, the wheel builder, Choose a Game, and the admin master-list form. The edit page also no longer opens the list on load.
- **Completing a DNF game refunds the DNF penalty**, and **DNF-ing a completed game takes back its completion tickets.** Both already happened through the edit form, but not through the Mark as Completed / Mark as DNF buttons, which assumed the game was Playing. `markAsCompleted` also refuses a game that's already completed, rather than awarding it twice.
- **WoW Retail and WoW Forever are told apart.** Both run `Wow.exe`, from `_retail_\` and `_forever_\`, and the tracker matched programs to games by exe name alone, so whichever was linked first claimed both: Retail was logged as Forever. Links are now **per folder**. An exe can have one link per folder (`game_executables.path_suffix`), and the tracker (v0.2.2) matches with `pick_link`:
  1. a link whose folder matches wins;
  2. otherwise a link saved before folders were recorded;
  3. a mismatched folder steps aside when the master list recognises the running path, so the real game gets identified and linked (how Retail gets its own link beside an existing Forever one);
  4. otherwise a single link still matches by name, as before (a reinstalled game, or a hand-linked one not on the list).

  The same fixes the Classic versions, which all share `WowClassic.exe`. Check-ins now carry the matched game's id (checked against your own library), and an exe with two links no longer breaks the name-only lookup that older trackers rely on (`pickLegacyLink`, `lib/exe-links.ts`). Every link write goes through `writeExeLink`. Tested live with both games installed: Forever → Forever, Retail → added as World of Warcraft with its own link, then each relaunch went straight to its own game with no prompts. SQL: `db/exe-links-per-folder.sql`, in two steps: add the per-folder index, deploy, then drop the old per-name constraint.

### Removed
- **The Videos page is gone.** It had been hidden from the menu and wasn't being used. Videos on a game's own page are unaffected and still stay in step with their YouTube playlist each day. Its old `videos` and `playlists` tables are removed by `db/drop-videos.sql`.

### Changed
- **No "Pick something to play" card on the dashboard.** With nothing in Playing (and, in the friends edition, nothing in Ambient), the dashboard's current-game card is left out altogether and the page starts with your stats. Both editions.
- **Activity rows never cut a chip off.** A day's unlocks (or backlog adds) now name as many as fit on the line, then "+N more" for the rest, recounting as the window changes size. Before, three were always named and the third (and the "+N more") could be cut off on a narrow screen. (`chipsThatFit`, `lib/activity-chips.ts`.)
- **Your week's bars widen with the card.** They were fixed at 28px, so a wider card just spread them further apart; now each fills most of its day (up to 48px).
- **Dashboard on medium screens: Friends and Your week sit side by side.** Once the right-hand cards drop below the main column, those two share a row (at matching heights) instead of each stretching across the page, which spread the week's chart out too far. They stack again on phones; on wide screens nothing changes.
- **The site's secret keys can't end up in the browser by mistake.** The code holding the master database key (and the IGDB, RAWG and YouTube keys) is now marked server-only, so accidentally using it from a page's browser code stops the build instead of quietly shipping. Nothing changes for anyone using the site.
- **Pages now refuse code that isn't the site's own.** Every page carries a Content-Security-Policy with a fresh one-time code (nonce), so a script only runs if the site itself put it there; code smuggled into a page (say, through a game title) can't run. YouTube videos, Turnstile and game art from other sites are allowed as before. Nothing should look or work differently. (`lib/csp.ts`, set in `proxy.ts`.)
- **Updated to the latest Next.js (16) and React (19).** Behind the scenes only: it closes a set of security holes in the older version, including serious ones in how images are served. Nothing should look or work differently.
- **The Stats page is redesigned, "Wrapped" style.** It's now a stack of bold, full-width colour bands, each built around one huge number, in a condensed display font (Anton). It opens on **your own stats**; a "Compare with a friend" dropdown switches to a **head-to-head**, and "Just me" switches back. Both views show the same sections in the same order:
  1. hours played
  2. games finished (with a per-year chart)
  3. finish rate
  4. **most played game**
  5. **longest session**
  6. **longest streak** (with your current streak)
  7. **tickets** earned and lost, broken down by where they came from and went, penalties in red
  8. highlights (longest clear, quickest, best month)
  9. **perfectionist** (games at 100%)
  10. level & XP
  11. genres

  Most played, longest session and longest streak are new and come from the desktop tracker's play sessions, on UK days. In the head-to-head, each stat is a round: its band is split between you (bright shade) and your friend (deep shade), sized by the result, with an AHEAD tag and a verdict. Tickets count as one round, decided on net. The **final score** (rounds won) comes last. On phones each band becomes two bars instead. Sections with no data are left out, such as the tracker sections for someone without the tracker, and a round only counts when both of you have data. The default is no longer comparing with your first friend. Numbers in `lib/stats-wrapped.ts`, components in `components/stats/wrapped/`.

### Fixed
- **Tracker: updating mid-game no longer loses time or splits the session.** Installing an update closes the tracker, and it used to do so without a final check-in, losing everything since the last one (up to 15 minutes); the restarted tracker then began a second session. Now:
  - the update downloads first, with tracking carrying on;
  - the session in progress is saved to disk and checked in;
  - then the installer runs.

  When the new version starts and the same game is still running (within 30 minutes), it **carries on the same session**: same start time, no new "Now tracking" notification, and the minutes include the update time, since the game never stopped. If the game was closed meanwhile, the saved session is closed off where it was left. If the site can't be reached, the check-in waits in the tracker's offline queue as usual. Logic in `resume_session` / `saved_final_payload` (`tracker.rs`); the saved session lives in `resume-session.json`.
- **Tracker: Quit from the tray ends the session properly.** It sends the same final check-in as closing a game before exiting, instead of dropping up to 15 minutes. It waits at most 5 seconds, so quitting can't hang while offline.
- **Tracker: the "Stopped tracking" notification shows the real session length.** It used to say "Xh added", but that was only what the *final* check-in added since the one before it. The tracker checks in every 15 minutes, so a 2-hour session could close with "0.1h added". It now reads e.g. "Mortal Kombat 1 — played 2h 5m this session, 32.1h in total." The total is left off until there's at least 0.1h to show. The hours saved on the website were always right; only the notification's wording was wrong. Notifications are now also written to the tracker's log.
- **Mark as Completed no longer wipes tracked hours.** The form's Hours Played and Achievement Progress boxes start empty, and every field is labelled optional. But leaving them blank erased the hours the desktop tracker had logged, and the completion tickets were then worked out from nothing. A blank box now keeps the game's current value. Found while testing the replay flow end to end: a replay with 3h tracked dropped to 0h on completion.

### Added
- **Tracker: "Track as Ambient" on the "is this a replay?" prompt.** For a game you've finished and keep playing for something other than a replay, such as Mortal Kombat 1's multiplayer after the story. It creates a separate Ambient entry and leaves the Completed one untouched (hours, date, tickets), and future launches track to that Ambient entry with no prompt. Needs the matching website change (`/api/agent/resume` accepting `status: "ambient"` for a Completed match) deployed first.
- **Tracker: "Not now" on a replay/resume prompt lasts for that launch of the game.** It covers all three prompts: Completed ("is this a replay?"), DNF ("are you resuming it?") and Backlog ("set it to…"). The prompt used to pop straight back up every few seconds while the game was open, because the identify step that raises it ignored the answer. Now "Not now" is tied to that game's running process. The tracker leaves that launch alone completely: no prompt, and no repeated calls to the site. Close the game and open it again, and it asks again, even if the tracker kept running.
- **"Playing Now" on the dashboard.** Shows everyone currently in a game, you and your friends: their avatar, the game (over its artwork, linking to the game page) and a ticking timer ("In game · 1h 23m"). You come first, then whoever has been playing longest. The list re-checks itself every minute while the tab is open, through a small new `/api/playing-now` endpoint, so people starting or stopping appear and disappear without a reload. The section is hidden when nobody's playing. "Live" uses the same rule as the game-card badge: an open session checkpointed in the last 20 minutes (`livePlayers` in `lib/presence.ts`).
- **Story, multiplayer and replays of one game now track to the right entry.** A game can have several entries: the Completed story run, an Ambient entry for the multiplayer played since, and later a Playing replay. The tracker routes (state, session, identify) now share one rule in `lib/trackable-row.ts`: hours go to the game's **Playing** entry, then its **Ambient** one. So:
  - a replay takes the hours while it's in progress, even when the exe was linked to the Ambient entry or the replay has no Steam id of its own;
  - once the replay is Completed, tracking falls back to the Ambient entry by itself, with no prompt and nothing to re-link.

  Previously Steam games went to whichever entry was edited last, and linked exes stuck to their old entry. This also works with the tracker version people already have installed.
- **The wheel creates a proper replay for a completed game that also has an Ambient entry.** It used to convert the Ambient entry into the Playing one, which:
  - carried all the multiplayer hours into the replay, inflating its completion tickets;
  - dropped the replay flag;
  - lost the Ambient entry.

  Now a Completed entry means a fresh replay starting from 0 hours. An Ambient entry for a game never completed is still promoted to Playing, as before.
- **"Track as Ambient" support for the replay prompt (website side).** `/api/agent/resume` accepts `status: "ambient"` for a Completed match, creating an Ambient entry (not a replay) for play after finishing a game, such as multiplayer. The tracker button ships in the next tracker release.
- **Play Sessions card: hover a session to see its day's total.** Each day's sessions form one block, so hovering highlights the whole day as a single shape and floats a small card above it ("Tue 3 Nov · 2h 10m · 3 sessions"). The total covers every session that day, even ones hidden behind "Show all". The card is positioned against the window, so the scrolling expanded list can't clip it, and it hides when you scroll.
- **Play Sessions card: long durations stay on one line.** A session like "14h 24m" was wider than the duration column, so it wrapped onto two lines. The column now grows to fit and never wraps.
- **Play Sessions card: the date appears once per day.** When a game was played more than once on a day, the date shows only on the first session. The rest sit under it with no divider, so each day reads as one group.
- **12 new Play Sessions achievements, earned from tracked play.** A new section on the Achievements page:
  - **Sessions tracked:** Press Start (1), Regular Visitor (50), Frequent Flyer (250), Season Ticket (1,000).
  - **Streaks:** Daily Driver (7 days in a row), Creature of Habit (30 days in a row).
  - **Big days:** Weekend Warrior (6h+ across one Saturday and Sunday), Bank Holiday (10h+ in one day).
  - **Variety:** Channel Surfer (3 games in one day), Wanderer (10 different games), One True Love (50h on one game).
  - **Social:** Squad Up (the same game at the same time as a friend, overlapping by at least 10 minutes).

  Only time the desktop tracker recorded counts, never hours typed in by hand. Days are UK days, and a session crossing midnight is split between the two. Sessions under a minute don't count. Each unlocks on the player's next tracker check-in, or on any save that already re-checks achievements, and anyone who already qualifies from past play unlocks it straight away. Rules in `lib/session-achievements.ts`.
- **Play Sessions card on every game page.** A plain list of every session the desktop tracker logged for the game, newest first. ### Changed
- **Avatar initials no longer include punctuation.** A display name like “Alex (test)” showed as “A(” on Recent Activity and the dashboard feed; it now reads “AT”, matching what the profile pages already did.
- **My Games stops looking half-loaded.** The skeleton was handing over to a page whose artwork hadn’t downloaded yet, so cards appeared as empty boxes with text floating on them. Cards and the profile banner now sit on a filled background from the first paint, so they read as placeholders until the art arrives. The banner’s screenshot lookup also no longer holds up the whole page — the list renders first and the backdrop follows.
- **Server moved next to the database.** The app’s functions were running in Washington DC while the database sits in Frankfurt, so every query on a page render crossed the Atlantic and back (~95ms each, several times per page). They now run in Frankfurt alongside it.
- **Pages open faster.** Every page load used to wait on the notification bell’s data and a changelog lookup before anything could render — four Supabase round trips stacked up before the first pixel, on every navigation. The nav and page now render as soon as your profile is loaded, and the bell streams in a beat later, so switching pages (the add-game form and games list especially) feels quicker. The bell also does two queries instead of three.
- **Wheel history reads as a timeline instead of a card grid.** Spins were laid out as cards in a grid, and a grid row stretches every card to match the tallest one in it — so a Choose turn with nothing but a result got padded out with a block of dead space to line up with a Spin & Reroll carrying a skipped-turn note, paid-action rows and a list of the other games on the wheel. It's now one continuous log where each entry is only as tall as what it has to show: a plain result is a single line, and the spend breakdown and wheel list appear underneath only when they exist. Your own spins stay tinted with your accent colour so they're still easy to pick out among everyone else's.

### Fixed
- **A game could be marked completed (or dropped) with a date in the future.** The date picker on the add/edit form and the "Mark as Completed" modal both now cap at today, and the server rejects a future date either way — it previously went straight to the database, which is how a completion landed on a date three weeks out. Recent Activity also showed that as "Just now": a future date makes the "how long ago" calculation go negative, which the formatter had no handling for — fixed too, so a future-dated row (old data, a clock skew) now shows the plain date instead.
- **Recent Activity re-announced "started playing" on every launch of an already-known ambient game.** Each launch writes its own session row, and every one of them was shown as "started playing" while in progress — so replaying Star Citizen for the fifth time said the same thing as the very first. It's now only said once per game, the first time; every session still shows "played for Xh Ym" once it ends, since that's real information worth repeating.
- **A trademark symbol in a title silently broke identification.** ®/™/© are common in official titles (“Bejeweled® 3”, “STAR WARS™ Battlefront™ II”) and IGDB's search doesn't transparently ignore them the way it handles case — a search containing one returned zero results even for a title IGDB has, so any EA/Epic game whose exact name carried one of these symbols silently fell back to the manual toast instead of auto-filing. They're stripped from what actually gets searched now; comparison was never affected, since it already ignores all punctuation.
- **Deleting an identified game to retest it left it unrecognisable for the rest of that session.** The desktop app remembers which executables it has already identified, so it does not ask the server about the same one on every single poll — but it was remembering successes exactly like failures. Delete the game afterward (to retest, say) and the link that made it recognisable goes with it, yet the app still believed the question was settled and never tried again, for as long as it stayed running. Only outcomes that genuinely do not change without you acting on the site are remembered now; a successfully identified game is free to be identified again the moment there is nothing left recognising it.
- **A game identified by name stayed recognisable only once.** Epic games were filed in correctly the first time, then went straight back to the "is this a game?" toast on the very next check — there was never any lasting record linking the executable to the game, only a one-off "don't ask again this run" flag, so the agent genuinely couldn't recognise it a second time. It now saves the same executable link a manual "Link a game" would, so once a game is identified, it stays identified. Ambient games added this way are also now correctly labelled by platform ("Steam", "Epic Games") instead of a flat "PC".
- **The link panel no longer silently suggests a wrong game.** Its Game dropdown had no "nothing selected" option, so whichever game happened to be listed first was shown as pre-chosen — which is what made the app appear to ask "is this Baldur's Gate 3?" about a completely unrelated game from another launcher. It now starts blank and has to be chosen on purpose.
- **You're asked about a new game as it opens, not three minutes later.** The desktop app waited three minutes before offering to link an unrecognised game — long enough that it felt broken. It now asks about twenty seconds in, while the game is still on its loading screen. Two things made that safe: programs that are part of installing or repairing a game (`setup`, `vcredist`, `unins`, `crashhandler` and friends) are skipped, since they sit in the same folders and load the same graphics runtimes as the game itself; and more launchers are ignored, because starting a game often puts its launcher on screen first and that's the wrong thing to be asked about.
- **A game was written off seconds after launching and never reconsidered.** The app remembered its verdict on every process to avoid inspecting the same ones repeatedly — but two of its checks are about how far into starting up a program is, not what it is. Caching those meant a game was judged "too new" on the first look and never examined again, so the three-minute wait never actually elapsed for anything; it just silenced the prompt permanently. Only settled verdicts are kept now, and a program still starting up gets another look on the next poll.
- **A question you never answered gets asked again.** The desktop app recorded a program as "asked about" the moment it raised the question, not when you answered it — so a toast you missed, or one lost because the app restarted, meant that program was never offered again. Only an outright **No / Not a game** is remembered now; a question that went unanswered comes back next time, and sending one to the link panel only stops it reappearing for the rest of that run. (If a game has stopped prompting you because of this, delete it from `asked.json` in the app's data folder.)
- **"Link a game" in the desktop app no longer offers a nonsense program.** The button handed its own click event to the link panel as the program to pre-select, so the picker opened with a bogus `[object PointerEvent]` entry at the top of the list — and, because it thought a program had already been chosen for you, skipped suggesting the real one.
- **The loading skeleton on My Games stays up until the list appears.** Opening My Games showed a blank gap instead of the skeleton: the page bounced from `/games` to `/games?status=completed&sort=completion_date`, and the loading state did not survive that redirect. Both My Games and friend profiles now apply the Completed default in place, so there is no redirect, no second server round trip, and the skeleton holds until the content is ready.
- **CSV-imported games now get box art.** The spreadsheet importer (`/import`) was already fetching a portrait cover image from RAWG/IGDB but throwing it away — only the landscape banner (`image_url`) was ever stored, never `cover_image_url`. Since Box Art is the default games-list view, every imported game rendered as a blank placeholder tile regardless of how good the enrichment match was. It now stores the cover too.
- **Wheel history's badges no longer change colour depending on who's looking.** "Spin" used `brand-*`, which follows the logged-in viewer's own accent colour — so the same badge could render as any hue depending on who opened the page. It's fixed blue now (the same blue `StatusBadge.tsx` uses for "Playing"), and "Choose" is teal — clear of Reroll/Skip's amber and the ticket-award emerald too. Fixing this surfaced a real bug along the way: `tailwind.config.ts`'s `content` globs never scanned `lib/`, where these badge classes actually live — any class here that didn't *also* happen to appear verbatim in an already-scanned file silently never got compiled at all, which is why an earlier emerald attempt looked washed out and a pink one rendered with no colour whatsoever. `lib/**` is now included.
- **"Games Completed Per Year" no longer strands its bars at opposite edges.** With only one or two years of history, the chart used to spread them evenly across the card's full width, leaving a big empty gap between them. It now sizes itself to roughly one slot per year — bars stay a consistent width and packed from the left — and only grows to the full card width once there are enough years to fill it.
- **Choosing a game on a Choose turn no longer reads as "spun the wheel" in Recent Activity.** A Choose turn never spins — you pick directly — but its history row was being lumped in with real spins, so the feed said "spun the wheel → <game>" either way. It now says "chose <game>", on your own profile, friends' profiles, the dashboard, and the site-wide Activity page alike.
- **No more pointless final spin on a one-game wheel.** When a paid Spin & Reroll's elimination run narrowed down to a single remaining game, it still made you click "Spin again" for a wheel with only one possible outcome. It now settles straight to the result, matching what the free spin and paid Spin Again already did.
- **The Entries list now matches the wheel.** Games taken off the wheel — whether eliminated this round, rejected by paying to spin again, or already locked in as a Spin & Reroll option — now show crossed out in the side list instead of looking like they're still in the running, and the count reads as remaining-of-total (e.g. "Entries 3/5"). The "N games remaining" line and the list are now driven by the same source, so they can't disagree.
- **The wheel could freeze completely mid-spin.** In elimination mode, repeatedly paying to Spin & Reroll (and its "spin for the next game" follow-ups) could hang the wheel animation forever with no error — the game it was supposed to land on had been silently excluded from its own set of options, so it could never find where to stop. Reachable by continuing to spend tickets on Spin Again / Spin & Reroll across several rounds, exactly as far as the tickets allowed.
- **Paid wheel spins could take your tickets and lose the result.** Spin Again (−3) and Spin & Reroll (−6) deducted tickets and then returned the game they picked *only* in the page's response — it was never saved anywhere. If the tab closed, the connection dropped, or the browser crashed before you finished the turn, the tickets were gone with nothing to show for them and no entry in your wheel history. Paid results are now recorded in the same moment the tickets are spent. Ticket deductions are also checked for failure now, instead of carrying on as though they'd worked.
- **A friend's profile "Now Playing" section was capped at 2 games.** It now shows every game they're currently playing, matching your own `/games` page.
- **Game completions weren't earning tickets.** The recent DB cleanup dropped `profiles.tickets_earned_total` as apparently unused (nothing in the app read it) — but the `award_tickets` Postgres function still wrote to it internally, so every ticket award started failing silently the moment that column was dropped. The column has been restored, and `awardCompletionTickets`/`reverseGameCompletionTickets` now surface RPC errors instead of swallowing them, so a failure like this is loud next time. Balances affected during the gap were corrected.
